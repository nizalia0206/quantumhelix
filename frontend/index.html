04 · Scientific limits

What this model can and can't tell you

Tunneling is random by nature. QuantumHelix ranks where tautomer-driven errors are more likely; it does not forecast individual events.

It can

  • Rank sites in a sequence by relative tunneling-driven tautomer propensity.
  • Show how barrier height, temperature and isotope mass change tunneling rates.
  • Separate quantum tunneling from classical thermal hopping at each site.
  • Give researchers a fast first pass before expensive QM/MM simulations.

It can't (yet)

  • Predict when or in which cell a specific mutation will happen.
  • Diagnose disease or estimate a person's health risk.
  • Account for repair enzymes and the replication machinery, which change the barrier.
  • Replace DFT-calibrated parameters: the barrier values here are illustrative.

Background: Slocombe, Sacchi & Al-Khalili, Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (2021); Winokan et al., Scientific Reports 13 (2023); Löwdin, Reviews of Modern Physics (1963).
