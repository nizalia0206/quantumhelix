Mutations start with a proton out of place

QuantumHelix simulates proton tunneling in DNA base pairs and ranks the base pairs of a DNA sequence with a simplified, illustrative model. It is a teaching and exploration tool, not a mutation predictor.

04 · Scientific limits

What this model can and can't tell you

Tunneling is random by nature. QuantumHelix ranks where tautomer-driven errors are more likely; it does not forecast individual events.

It can

  • Rank sites in a sequence by relative tunneling-driven tautomer propensity.
  • Show how barrier height, temperature and isotope mass change tunneling rates.
  • Separate quantum tunneling from classical thermal hopping at each site.
  • Help people explore how tunnelling depends on barrier, temperature and mass.

It can't (yet)

  • Predict when or in which cell a specific mutation will happen.
  • Diagnose disease or estimate a person's health risk.
  • Account for repair enzymes and the replication machinery, which change the barrier.
  • Replace DFT-calibrated parameters: the barrier values here are illustrative.

Background: Slocombe, Sacchi & Al-Khalili, Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (2021); Winokan et al., Scientific Reports 13 (2023); Löwdin, Reviews of Modern Physics (1963).

05 · Our investigation

Our Investigation: Do the Hotspots Hold Up?

An exploratory computational study. We change the model's own assumptions over thousands of scenarios and measure how much the predicted hotspot ranking moves. It tests the mathematics of this model, not real DNA.

Research question and hypotheses

Question. How robust are the predicted proton-tunnelling hotspots when the physical assumptions of the model change?

H1. The rank order of sites is robust to ±20–50 % changes in barrier height, width, temperature and tautomer asymmetry.
H2. The hotspot list (the app's rule: rescaled score ≥ 0.85) is less stable than the rank order.

Honest history: H2 and the "stress" study were added after the first run showed the rank order never changed. This is exploratory analysis, not a pre-registered test.

What we changed, and what we held fixed

Held constant: the equations below, proton mass (the deuteron is only a separate sanity check), the 0.85 hotspot cutoff, one proton in a 1-D double well, no cellular environment. Ranges are ±20–50 % around the illustrative baselines. They are not literature-derived.

Methods and equations (existing physics engine, unchanged)

V(x) = V0 ((x/a)^2 - 1)^2
w = sqrt(8 V0 / (m a^2)),  E0 = hbar w / 2
T = exp( -(2/hbar) * integral sqrt(2m(V-E0)) dx )   (WKB)
k_tun = (w/2pi) T,  k_th = (w/2pi) exp(-(V0-E0)/kB T)
f = 1 / (1 + exp(asym / kB T))      (tautomer fraction)
site score = log10[ (k_tun + k_th) f ]
V0 shifted -0.010 eV per A-T neighbour, +0.004 eV per G-C neighbour

Experimental design

  • Four synthetic 60 bp sequences (AT-rich, balanced, GC-rich, AT block + GC block). They are not real genes.
  • One at a time: each parameter swept over 5 values.
  • Global: 1,000-scenario Latin hypercube over all six parameters, run twice: plausible ranges and wider "stress" ranges.
  • Metrics vs the baseline ranking: Kendall τ-b and Spearman ρ (tie-aware), hotspot-set Jaccard overlap, and whether G–C or A–T ranks higher on average. Also 3,000 scenario-vs-scenario comparisons that do not use the baseline.
  • Which assumption matters: first-order sensitivity index S1 (binned estimate).
  • Seed 20261021. Scores are rounded to 10⁻⁹ before ranking so floating-point noise cannot invent distinctions.

τ-b = 1 means the same order as the baseline; 0 means unrelated; negative means reversed. "Stable" here means τ-b ≥ 0.8, a threshold we chose. Jaccard = overlap of hotspot sets (1 = identical).

Finding 1 · The rank order is almost unbreakable, and that is a property of the model, not a discovery

In the plausible-range study, τ-b ≥ 0.8 in – of scenarios for every sequence. Barrier height, width, temperature, tautomer asymmetry and the A–T offset never reordered the sites. The order is fixed by built-in signs: G–C outranks A–T because its tautomer is cheaper, A–T neighbours lower the barrier, G–C neighbours raise it. Base identity alone already reproduces much of the ranking (τ-b with a plain G/C indicator: –). Each sequence has only – distinct score levels out of 60, so most sites are tied.

Finding 2 · The hotspot list is much less stable than the ranking

Because scores are rescaled to 0–1 and cut at 0.85, the hotspot set depends on the spread between best and worst site, not just their order. Median overlap with the baseline hotspot set ranges from – across sequences in the plausible study. A stable rank order therefore does not mean a stable hotspot call.

Finding 3 · The least-justified assumption matters most

The neighbour (stacking) shift size explains the most variation in hotspot overlap (S1 = – in the plausible study). Those −0.010 / +0.004 eV shifts are modelling assumptions, and we found no literature value for them. With the shift set to zero, the ranking collapses to a plain A–T versus G–C split.

Finding 4 · Under stress, the ranking can break

With wider ranges (including a reversed neighbour effect), up to – of scenarios reverse the ranking for the worst-affected sequence (τ-b below 0). A–T only outranks G–C when its barrier is lowered by roughly – eV, far outside the plausible range. Doubling the mass (deuteron check) changes rates but not the ranking.

Inconclusive

Four short synthetic sequences cannot say how this behaves on real genomes. The baseline itself is illustrative, which is why we also report scenario-vs-scenario comparisons that do not depend on it. A ranking that is stable under changes to a model's parameters can still be wrong if the model's structure is wrong.

What the results establish

  • Mathematical sensitivity: how this simplified model's rankings respond to its own parameters.
  • Within the plausible ranges, rank order does not depend on barrier height, width, temperature or asymmetry; hotspot calls depend mostly on the neighbour-shift assumption.
  • The scanner's output is largely determined by local base composition.

What they do not establish

  • Biological validity. A stable ranking is not a validated prediction.
  • Mutation rates, disease risk or clinical meaning.
  • That real DNA behaves like these synthetic sequences or that the baseline parameters are right.
  • Novelty. We have not shown a new scientific result.

Comparison with published work (only where comparable)

Comparable in direction only. Slocombe, Al-Khalili & Sacchi (2021, doi:10.1039/D0CP05781A) model A–T and G–C proton transfer with density functional theory and machine-learning nudged-elastic-band methods. A talk summary by one of the authors says lasting changes look unlikely for A–T but more significant for G–C. Our baseline also ranks G–C above A–T, but here that order comes straight from the illustrative asymmetry values we chose (0.10 vs 0.20 eV), so this is not an independent validation.

Not comparable. Slocombe, Sacchi & Al-Khalili (2022, doi:10.1038/s42005-022-00881-8) report, for G–C in an open-quantum-systems model, that tunnelling contributes more than classical hopping. Our toy model gives a tunnelling share of about 5 % for a 0.36 eV barrier. Different physics (double proton transfer, environment, DFT barriers), so we cannot say which is closer. It is one more reason to treat our absolute rates as illustrative.

06 · Next steps

How we would improve this research

Each step keeps the same physics core and replaces an illustrative assumption with something that can be checked.

Now · Prototype

Simplified model

1-D double well, WKB tunnelling, live Schrödinger simulator, sensitivity study.

Next

Literature-derived energy landscapes

Replace the illustrative barriers and neighbour shifts with values calculated or measured for each base pair and sequence context.

Then

Environment effects

Add the surroundings (water, enzymes such as polymerase and helicase) that change the barrier and the tautomer lifetime.

Then

Validation

Compare outputs with published computational or experimental results for the same quantities and conditions. No such validation has been done yet.

Limit that remains: even a well-calibrated molecular model does not by itself give a biological mutation rate, because replication fidelity and repair also decide whether a rare tautomer becomes a mutation.
